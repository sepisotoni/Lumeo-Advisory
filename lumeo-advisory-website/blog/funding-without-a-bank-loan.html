Funding without a bank loan: crowdfunding, invoice finance and supplier terms

A bank loan is not the only route, and for many small businesses it is not the available one. These alternatives suit different situations.

Options to consider

  • Crowdfunding. Platforms such as Thundafund let supporters back a product or project. It works best when you have an audience and a clear reward to offer.
  • Invoice finance. A funder advances part of what a customer owes you and collects when they pay. It frees cash but costs more than a loan.
  • Supplier terms. Negotiate 30 or 60 days instead of cash on delivery. It is free, if you pay on time.
  • Customer deposits. Ask for a deposit on larger orders so you can buy materials.
  • Equipment finance. Spread the cost of vehicles or machinery over time.
  • Development finance from SEFA, NEF or Business Partners, which can be cheaper than commercial credit.

Check the real cost

Compare offers by the total you will pay back, not the headline fee. A short-term advance with a small fee can work out expensive when you annualise it. Ask for the repayment schedule in writing.

Check the lender

Credit providers must be registered. Look up any lender on the National Credit Regulator site before you share bank details or sign. Be wary of anyone who asks for a payment before a loan is approved.

Plan the repayment first

Before you borrow, show on paper how repayments fit into cash flow in your slowest month. If they do not, adjust the amount or choose a different route.
